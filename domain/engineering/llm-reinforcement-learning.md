# LLM Reinforcement Learning

## Boundary of the term

Reinforcement learning is a learning process in which an agent interacts with an environment and seeks to maximize cumulative reward. For an LLM system, the defining operation is a policy update driven by reward or preference-derived signal; storing feedback, choosing a deterministic next step, retrying a prompt, or routing tools does not by itself update a policy and therefore is not RL. [Sutton and Barto](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262039246/reinforcement-learning/)

Use precise labels:

| Mechanism | Changes model policy? | Correct label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Store accept/reject/correction events | No | Feedback logging or evidence capture |
| Select a next task from explicit rules | No | Deterministic orchestration |
| Sample several answers and keep the verified one | No | Inference-time search or reranking |
| Fine-tune on demonstrations | Yes | Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) |
| Fit a model to ranked outputs | Changes reward estimator, not yet the acting policy | Reward/preference modeling |
| Optimize the acting model against a learned or programmatic reward | Yes | Reinforcement learning |
| Optimize a policy directly from preference pairs with DPO | Yes | Direct preference optimization; it solves the RLHF objective without an explicit online RL loop |

The distinction matches InstructGPT’s three separate stages—SFT, reward-model training from ranked samples, and PPO policy optimization—and DPO’s explicit replacement of reward-model fitting plus RL sampling with a classification objective. [InstructGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155) [DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)

## RLHF and reward modeling

A common RLHF pipeline samples model outputs, asks humans to rank them, trains a reward model to predict those preferences, and optimizes the language-model policy against that learned reward while penalizing excessive movement from a reference policy. InstructGPT used this pipeline after supervised instruction tuning and optimized the policy with PPO. [InstructGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)

PPO is a policy-gradient family that alternates between sampling interaction data and taking multiple minibatch optimization steps on a clipped surrogate objective. It is an optimizer, not the source of truth for whether a reward encodes the desired behavior. [PPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347)

Reward models are proxies. Optimizing a policy too strongly against a proxy can improve the proxy score while degrading the underlying target; controlled experiments in reward-model overoptimization found scaling relationships between optimization pressure and divergence from a gold reward model. [Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760)

## RLAIF and constitutional feedback

RLAIF replaces some human preference judgments with judgments generated by another model. Constitutional AI uses written principles to produce critiques, revisions, and AI preference labels, then trains a preference model and an RL policy; the human contribution includes the governing principles rather than per-example harmfulness labels. [Constitutional AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073)

Google’s RLAIF study compared AI and human feedback on summarization, helpful dialogue, and harmless dialogue, and also evaluated direct rewards from an off-the-shelf LLM. Its reported parity is scoped to those models, tasks, and evaluations; AI-generated labels do not become independent ground truth because they are cheaper or more scalable. [RLAIF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00267)

## Verifiable rewards and policy optimization

A verifiable reward is computed from an external check with a determinate result, such as an exact answer rule, compiler, test, or proof checker. This narrows ambiguity relative to a learned preference proxy, but the verifier still defines the optimized target: weak tests and exploitable graders create weak rewards. DeepSeekMath used outcome and process supervision for mathematical tasks and introduced Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which estimates relative advantages from groups of sampled outputs rather than relying on PPO’s separate value model. [DeepSeekMath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300)

DPO starts from preference pairs and parameterizes the optimal policy so training can use a binary classification loss without explicitly fitting a reward model or sampling from the policy during RL fine-tuning. It belongs in the preference-learning toolbox, but calling every DPO dataset or update “online reinforcement learning” erases the algorithmic distinction the paper introduced. [DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)

## Engineering contract

Before claiming RL, record the policy revision before and after training, the data-generation policy, the feedback source, the reward or preference transformation, the optimization algorithm, and held-out evaluation. InstructGPT, Constitutional AI, RLAIF, DPO, and DeepSeekMath differ on these exact axes; “uses feedback” is too weak to identify the method. [InstructGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155) [Constitutional AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073) [RLAIF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00267) [DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290) [DeepSeekMath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300)

Keep production feedback capture append-only and auditable, then train offline from a versioned snapshot. Evaluate the resulting policy against held-out task quality, regressions, safety constraints, and resource cost before promotion. This placement prevents a logging system or deterministic task compass from being mistaken for a learner and makes the actual policy update reviewable against the reward-proxy risks documented in RLHF experiments. [InstructGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155) [Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760)

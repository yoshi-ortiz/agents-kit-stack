# 48-Hour Startups Hackathon: Execution & Methodology Guide

This framework strips away theoretical fluff, forcing teams to confront real-world market dynamics, ego management, and rapid execution.

## Friday: Market Reality & Team Formation

| Schedule Item | Domain Argument & Methodology Source | Startup Antipattern to Avoid | 
 | ----- | ----- | ----- | 
| **10:00 AM MASTERCLASS I: Rockstar Senior / Trends** | **Market Reality Check:** Setting the baseline by highlighting actual industry pain points.   *Methodology:* **Paul Graham (Y Combinator)** - "Make something people want." Focus on finding a real problem, not riding a hype cycle. | **The Hammer Looking for a Nail:** Forcing a trendy technology into a non-existent problem just to use a modern stack. | 
| **PLAYBOOK: Investment Simulator** | **Market-Driven Formation:** A "Stocks simulator" game with fake money acting as a democratic filter. Participants "invest" in pitches; only the top-funded ideas secure a table and a final demo slot.   *Methodology:* **Customer Validation (Steve Blank)** - Using simulated capital as a proxy for market demand and traction before writing a single line of code. | **The Infinite TAM:** Pitching a "Trillion Dollar Market" without being able to convince the person sitting next to them to hand over a fake \$100 bill. | 
| **FEEDBACK I: Collaborating with Others** | **Ego Triage & Talent Matching:** Addressing "CEO Syndrome." Unfunded idea-generators learn they must join forces as builders, while funded "CEOs" realize they lack the technical skills to execute alone.   *Methodology:* **Execution Multiplier (Derek Sivers)** - "Ideas are just a multiplier of execution." Forming teams based on complementary execution skills, not just shared enthusiasm. | **The Lone Wolf / Idle CEO:** An "idea person" refusing to touch code or talk to customers, trying to act as a pure manager on day one. | 
| **BANQUETE I: Support Network** | **Ecosystem Building:** High-quality food breaks forcing teams to step away, recharge, and cross-pollinate. | **Tunnel Vision:** Skipping meals to write boilerplate code, missing the vital peer-review benefits of the event. | 
| **PLAYBOOK: Market-fit Canvas (Lean MVP)** | **Value Proposition Definition:** Scoping the absolute minimum feature set required to test the riskiest assumption.   *Methodology:* **The Lean Startup (Eric Ries)** - The Build-Measure-Learn feedback loop starts with identifying the core hypothesis. | **The Kitchen Sink MVP:** Bloating the day-one scope with nice-to-have features instead of validating the core transaction. | 
| **FEEDBACK II: Lean MVP** | **Scope Triage:** Mentors brutally trim bloated product scopes to ensure testable prototypes by Saturday. | **Mentor Shopping:** Defending a bloated feature list and ignoring pushback until finding a mentor who blindly agrees. | 
| **Development** | **Execution Sprint 1:** Heads-down focused work to build the core architecture. | **Premature Optimization:** Setting up complex microservices instead of rapid prototyping. | 
| **MASTERCLASS II: E-commerce Sales** | **Problem-Centric Acquisition:** A crash course on consultative sales. Not about aggressively taking people's money, but discovering how to solve their pain points effectively to acquire them as users.   *Methodology:* **Customer Development (Steve Blank) / SPIN Selling** - Selling through inquiry, active listening, and mapping solutions directly to explicit customer needs. | **"If We Build It, They Will Come":** Assuming clean code automatically translates into user adoption without a dedicated customer discovery strategy. | 
| **BANQUETE II: Group Support** | **Mental Reset:** Evening dinner to decompress, share blockers, and prevent fatigue. | **Siloed Stress:** Internalizing team conflicts instead of leveraging the event's support network. | 
| **PLAYBOOK: UX Iteration** | **Friction Reduction:** Refining the user journey to ensure the "Aha!" moment is accessible immediately. | **Pixel-Perfect Paralysis:** Obsessing over perfectly rounded corners while the core user flow remains broken. | 
| **Development** | **Execution Sprint 2:** Implementing UI/UX feedback and locking in the primary user loop. | **Scope Creep:** Adding a massive new feature late on Friday night. | 
| **MASTERCLASS IV: Design / Branding / Public Speaking** | **Narrative Shaping:** Structuring a pitch that connects user pain, the solution, and market evidence. | **The GitHub Pitch:** Spending 4 minutes showing architecture diagrams instead of explaining business value. | 
| **FEEDBACK III: Pitch** | **Story Validation:** A dry run of the pitch narrative, exposing confusing messaging. | **Defensive Presenting:** Arguing with mentors over feedback rather than iterating on communication. | 

## Saturday: The Pivot, Validation, and Shipping

| Schedule Item | Domain Argument & Methodology Source | Startup Antipattern to Avoid | 
 | ----- | ----- | ----- | 
| **BANQUETE III: Group Support** | **Morning Alignment:** Setting the tempo for the final 10 hours and realigning teams after a night of rest. | **Unstructured Chaos:** Waking up and immediately writing code without a morning standup. | 
| **MASTERCLASS III: Agile & The Pivot** | **Rapid Realignment:** Teaching teams how to salvage their weekend if their initial premise failed overnight. How to ruthlessly triage, pivot the product direction based on Friday's evidence, and adapt their remaining hours.   *Methodology:* **The Pivot (Eric Ries) / Agile Manifesto** - "Responding to change over following a plan." Recognizing that changing a fundamental business hypothesis is a success of validation, not a failure. | **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** Blindly continuing to build a product that Friday's feedback proved nobody wants, simply because "we already wrote the code." | 
| **FEEDBACK IV: Market Share Proof** | **Evidence Check:** Forcing teams to show actual market validation—signups, waitlists, customer interviews, or mock transactions.   *Methodology:* **Traction (Gabriel Weinberg) / The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick)** - Gathering hard, empirical evidence of user intent that goes beyond polite compliments from friends and family. | **Vanity Metrics:** Presenting "number of lines of code written" or "website visits" instead of actual user commitments or conversions. | 
| **Development** | **Final Sprint:** The absolute last block for locking down code and prepping the live environment. | **The 11th Hour Refactor:** Pushing major database schema changes right before the demo deadline. | 
| **BANQUETE: Rest** | **Mandatory Downtime:** Forcing teams to step away to ensure they have the energy to deliver a compelling presentation. | **Panic Coding:** Continuing to code through the meal, guaranteeing a low-energy pitch. | 
| **PLAYBOOK: Handoff** | **Production Freeze:** Transitioning from "builder mode" to "presenter mode." Finalizing assets and freezing the code repository.   *Methodology:* **Ship It (Seth Godin) / Y Combinator Demo Day constraints** - Shipping imperfect products on a hard deadline to force prioritization and overcome perfectionism. | **"Works on My Machine":** Failing to test the live application on the presentation network, leading to a broken live demo. | 
| **7:00 PM: Startup Demos** | **The Culmination:** 10 teams pitch. Demonstrating a functional, evidence-backed prototype that solves a validated problem.   *Methodology:* **Do Things That Don't Scale (Paul Graham)** - Showcasing the MVP (even if the backend is heavily manual or "Wizard of Oz" style) to prove the core business concept and user demand to the judges. | **The Vaporware Pitch:** Delivering a beautiful 5-minute slide deck with zero actual product demonstration, live interaction, or evidence of execution. |
